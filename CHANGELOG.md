# Changelog

What changes in Safience from one version to the next, newest first. A change that fixes or adds something adds its line under **Unreleased** the day it is merged.

## Unreleased

- The command bar searches only the space you are in: another space's tabs no longer show there, as its history and bookmarks already didn't. Other spaces still show by name, to switch to.

- On iPhone and iPad, website dialogs now reply safely when dismissed without a button tap or when they cannot be shown, preventing a WebKit crash.

- Count and log history clearings that cannot be read from storage.

- Track and log workspace save failures until a later save succeeds.

### Fixed

- On the Mac, the Debug build's picture-in-picture test calls into its page through Page.swift, as every call into a page must, so the page-call policy test passes again.
- A download that can't start because its temporary folder can't be created now says why, instead of stopping without a message.
- Log failures to read or save iCloud sync state.


- Settings shows whether Safience is the default browser as soon as you answer macOS's dialog, however long it stays open.
- When history fails to save a change and also fails to undo it, the error now reports both failures instead of only the first.
- Check native extension host registry access on the main actor.

- Log the reason when retrying a workspace save fails on Mac.


- Site icons drawn as SVG no longer stop loading when the page that draws them closes unexpectedly.
- Log failures to save the installed extension list and extension records.


- On the Mac, a problem report that can't be saved now shows an alert with the reason instead of failing silently.

## 1.0.7

### Fixed

- Safience now shows a progress window while moving browser data from an older sandboxed installation. Large migrations no longer block the app from opening a window.
- Interrupted migration resumes from saved checkpoints and preserves the current workspace. Existing site data is kept, and partial copies are checked before being reused.
- Pages resize to their visible viewport when the sidebar or page container changes size. Split panes each receive their own viewport width.


## 1.0.6

### Fixed

- If the workspace cannot be opened, Safience now offers recovery options instead of replacing it. Restoring a backup preserves the original workspace and retained browsing data first.
- Moving a tab between spaces no longer reuses another space's cached page state. Restored tabs also reject stale snapshots after navigation.
- Tabs with recognizable unsaved form or editor content stay open during automatic memory cleanup. Some editors cannot be detected, and a terminated web process can still lose unsaved content.
- Removing a pin on another device keeps the local page open. History deletion confirms its scope, including visits hidden by filters and synced devices.
- Setup can resume in another iPad window after its original window closes. Background Mac pages cannot interrupt other windows with JavaScript dialogs.
- Improved accessibility for the Mac command bar, bookmarks popovers, recovery actions and larger text. Tab transitions respect Reduce Motion.
- Improved picture in picture video selection, pinned tab press feedback and copied-link tracking cleanup.

## Earlier development notes

These notes are retained from the development log and may describe features shipped before 1.0.6.

### Review fixes

- Unpinning on another device keeps the local tab open, with its page, title and selection; it no longer closes local work. Repeated sync does not recreate the removed pin.
- History search shows the latest visit inside its time and device filters. Deleting a page now asks first, and page, day and range clearings explain that hidden visits and synced devices are included.
- Bookmarks popovers identify themselves as modal to accessibility, focus their close control and support the VoiceOver escape action. Action toasts adapt to larger text, repeated copies get distinct feedback, and enabling blocking describes the setting rather than guaranteeing every ad was blocked.
- A cancelled pinned-button press clears its pressed state. The pinned list and its rows settle within 300 ms, with a smaller button transition and quicker repeated highlight changes.
- Copied and shared links remove site-specific tracking tags from fully qualified hosts ending in a dot. Search suggestion disclosures now explain that a partial address may be sent before it is recognized.
- Automatic picture in picture ranks videos by their visible viewport area and excludes hidden or transparent videos, so a larger offscreen video does not replace the one being watched.

Settings shows only what an iPhone uses: whether the address bar shows the page's address or its title, without the iPad's layout, tab bar, trackpad and shortcut settings.
- The menu of the tab on screen has what the phone's page menu (…) has: Share Page, Bookmark This Page or Remove Bookmark, Pin or Unpin Tab, and Commands and Settings at the end, beside the tab's own items, in groups.
- New Tab, Close Tab, Reopen Closed Tab, New Window and Close Window are on ⌘T, ⌘W, ⇧⌘T, ⌘N and ⇧⌘W, and the next and previous tab on ⌃⇥ and ⌃⇧⇥, as in every browser and tabbed app, in place of their ⌃⌥ chords: these are the keys browsers keep from pages (Chrome reserves them), so no page counts on them. Every other shortcut stays on ⌃⌥.
- The iPad's menu bar has one Window menu: iPadOS's own, which shows whatever an app does, with Show or Hide Tab Bar, Diagnostics and Give Keys Back to the Page at its top and its items' ⌘ keys taken off. Settings… (⌃⌥,) moved to the Safience menu, above iPadOS Settings…, iPadOS's item that opens Safience's page in the Settings app.
- Only the tab on screen is in Liquid Glass; every other tab is flat, with a faint see-through shade under the pointer that takes on the bar's colour.
- Tapping or clicking the address selects all of it, so typing replaces it, as in Safari.
- Bars are 44 points tall, so every control answers in at least 40 by 40, and presses give a little (to 0.96).
- A link opened from another app opens in a new tab after the one on screen, instead of replacing that tab's page.
- The address bar shows on every page, so the address and the way back stay in view. Settings › Show the address bar can keep it to sign-in pages, as before.
- On iPhone, the bar's motion and touch, after a design engineering review: a sideways swipe catches a slide still settling and goes on from where it is, with a tap as it crosses the line past which it goes; the address row's spring starts at the finger's speed; the tab sheet answers touches as it lands, and Escape puts it away at once; presses go down in 0.12 seconds; the star and reload change by the system's symbol replace; the loading line is scaled rather than resized and finishes before it fades; quieter words are a little darker, for contrast; buttons are 44 points. The address shows the host alone, cut from its start when long, as Safari's. Reduce Motion fades what would slide.

### Fixed

- On iPhone, the swipes on the bar (up for the tabs, sideways to the tab beside) could stop working until the app was quit. The bar takes note that a swipe is under way as it lets one begin; one stopped by the system before it began, which tells the app nothing, left that note behind, and every swipe after it was turned down. The note is now cleared when the swipe lets go of its touches, however it ended (`BarSwipe.reset`).
- A pinned tab whose icon is all but black (Vercel's disc, Mobbin's mark) sank into its own glow while on screen, and Mobbin's mark, which is that black throughout, vanished: the glow is now only a hint of the colour, the tile's glass is tinted faintly, and the icon shows whole over it; the same for an icon all but white on a dark screen. An icon all of one colour is drawn in white or black, whichever stands out, on screen, and at rest too where it is the colour of the screen (Mobbin's black mark in Dark Mode). iPad and Mac (`PinnedIcon`, `IconTint.knockout`).
- Google's search pages, and any site that renders light or dark on its server, were light on a dark screen, however often they were loaded: they ask for the screen's look as a header (`Sec-CH-Prefers-Color-Scheme`, named in `Accept-CH`), which Chromium sends and WebKit never does. Safience sends it to the hosts that ask, and only to them, on iPhone, iPad and Mac (`ColorSchemeHint`); a page that came light on a dark screen is asked for again, dark, once. The hosts are kept for the run, never written down. A step back, a reload and a form sent by POST go as they were made.

- On iPhone, an icon scrolled to either end of the pinned tabs' dock, in the tab overview, showed past the dock's rounded ends: the glass draws the capsule but cuts nothing on it to its shape. The row of icons is now cut to the same capsule (`PinnedDock`). 1.0.1 had this fix and 1.0.2's first build did not.
- Handoff could give an app the file and then write it again under it. An app picked just as Apple Intelligence's answer came was given the file between the check that no app had it and the writing of the better one, and could read either. The check and the writing are now one step, under the lock the share sheet sets (`HandoffJob.rewrite`).
- Two handoffs begun close together, in two windows, could leave the second without its file: the first, emptying the shelf of handoffs that are over, went by a list of those under way taken before the second had begun, and took the second's folder away. Which are under way is now asked, behind a lock, as each old folder is looked at (`HandoffJob.live`).
- A page whose title is mostly emoji (70 of them, or 30 emoji families) handed off its question and address only. The file's name was cut at 80 characters, and a file system counts a name in bytes, 255 of them: the file could not be written. The name is now also cut to 240 bytes, between whole characters, which keeps 80 characters of Hangul whole (`Handoff.fileName`). The joiners that hold an emoji of several together (a family) and some scripts' letters stay in a name as well: they were taken for control characters, and the emoji came apart with spaces between.
- A filter's `:style()` was given to an element once: a page that set the element's style anew, as one that draws the element again does, lost it for good. Each pass now gives it again where the page has set it otherwise (`blocker.js`).
- `no-xhr-if` kept answering a request with nothing once it had matched: a page that opened the same request again for something the filter doesn't name had that answered with nothing too, with the empty answer's status and text left on it. A request opened again for something else now goes, and is answered (`scriptlets.js`).
- On iPhone, the bottom of a page stayed under the bar. The page runs on under the bar, which floats over it, and WebKit was told how much of it the bar covers (`obscuredContentInsets`); by that it lays the page out in what shows, and still stops its scrolling at the view's own bottom. So the last 138 points of every page were under the bar on an iPhone 17 Pro Max: a page's last line, and whatever it ends on (a form's button, a list's "more", the links at its foot). The scroll view is now given that room to scroll by as well (`Page.obscure`), and a page's end comes up over the bar. A page that only fills the window had nothing to scroll by at all: one built to the window's height (`height: 100vh` or `min-height: 100vh`, which WebKit gives as the whole view's height, bar and all, and for which a web view has no setting), or one a little shorter than the window. A chat's field, an editor's tools, a sign-in page's foot were under the bar, to be neither seen nor pressed. Such a page now stands clear of the bar (`FootRoom`, `Page.standsClear`): it is laid out in the window less the bar, 0.3 seconds after it has come to fill the window and no more, with the colour of its last points under the bar in place of its foot, and it runs under the bar again at once when it grows past the window. Told by the page's height alone, with no script in the page, so a hands-off page is treated the same. `100svh` and `100lvh` are also given as what shows above the bar (`setMinimumViewportInset`); they were the whole view's. Measured on a page of our own in the simulator: the bottom row of a `100vh` page at 851 to 894 points before (the page shows down to 756) and 713 to 756 after, where it takes a press; a long page's end at 851 to 894 before and 713 to 756 after. What is not changed: something a long page fixes in place by `100vh` (a sheet over the page with its button at `100vh`) still has its bottom under the bar.
- Scrolling a page that is laid out as an app, where the app scrolls the page for the wheel (the iPhone's desktop view, and an iPad's trackpad with the wheel bridge). A wheel's scroll goes to what is under the cursor and can scroll, as in a desktop browser, and on four kinds of page it found nothing or went wrong. A page whose root has an overflow set and whose body has a height (`html, body { height: 100%; overflow-x: hidden }`, as many an app built with React has) scrolls inside its body: the bridge took the body for the window, which has nowhere to go there, and the page stood still (`bridge.js bodyScrolls`). What a page puts in a component's slot is inside the scroller the component keeps round the slot, and the way up from it went past that scroller (`parentOf`, which the hover styles go by too). A scroller that must snap to its parts (`scroll-snap-type: y mandatory`, a page of full-screen sections) went on a part for every small step of a stroke, so one stroke was the end of the page: a stroke is now one turn, by most of the scroller's own size, which is one part of such a page and a few cards of a row of them (`turn`), and one way only where it snaps both ways. And in the iPhone's desktop view the cursor pushed against the view's bottom or top rests on whatever bar the page keeps there, a chat's field or an app's header, with nothing under it to scroll, where the push was meant to bring up what is further along: the scroll now goes to the nearest thing in the cursor's column that can take it (`reach`), up and down only, and only where the cursor is over nothing that scrolls at all, so a list at its end keeps the scroll. What can take it may be a frame's own document, in a page that keeps its matter in a frame under its bar, or the page itself, round a frame of its own that the cursor was left in and that has nothing to scroll (round another site's frame the page had the scroll already). A page the bridge is not in, a PDF, had nothing to scroll it in the desktop view at all, where the fingers are the trackpad's and not the page's: it is scrolled as its own view is (`Page.nudge`). Each of the first four is checked in `Tests/bridge.html`, in WebKit. In the simulator, in desktop view, on a page of our own (a header, a list of 150 rows, a bar at the bottom): three pushes of the cursor against the bottom, where it rests on the page's bar, took the list 1,256 points down in one run and 1,454 in another (the glide after a flick counted), and pushes against the top, on the header, brought it back to 0. A PDF of six pages went from its first page to its second by the same pushes.
- The first tap on the address, on iPhone, in a run of the app. The keyboard takes the system a moment to start the first time, and the app made that moment longer and showed it badly: the bar shrank to ride over the keyboard at the tap and then sat at the foot of the screen, its tools cut off, until the keyboard came. Three things are different. The bar keeps its place, tools and all, until the keyboard says it is coming and goes up with it (`Browser.keyboardHasStarted`, `WindowModel.barRidesKeyboard`); once the keyboard has come up in a run it is quick, and the bar makes room at the tap as before. The address is selected while the field takes the keys, not a turn later: selecting once the keyboard was the field's had UIKit wait on the main thread for the keyboard to finish starting, a third of what that first tap cost there, with nothing moving meanwhile (`TypingField`). And Apple Intelligence's model is no longer looked up at the tap: on the main thread it came before the keyboard was asked for and was a tenth of the tap (see Changed). An empty field (the + button) selects nothing, which waited the same way for no reason. Measured in the simulator on a Mac under heavy load, so as shares of the main thread's time, not as times.
- A window that turned phone-width or back while an address was being typed ended the typing and left the keys with no one: a keyboard's keys went nowhere until the page was tapped. They go back to the page, as they do however else typing ends (`Browser.refreshChrome`).
- Apple Intelligence in the address bar offered tabs, bookmarks or commands for whatever was typed, a plain web search included: asked to pick from the list, Apple's model always picks something ("weather tomorrow" got an open Google Flights tab), and it filled the three it was allowed. It is now asked in two steps. Its picks, up to five, are only what the words might mean, each given by name as well as number, which also stops it landing a line off in a long list; then each pick is asked about by itself, graded as asked for, related or unrelated, and only what isn't unrelated is offered. So the rows it adds are as many as fit, from none to five. Tried against the model on a Mac with 38 phrases: all 15 plain web searches got no row from it (none of 15 before), and of 13 rows it added one was beside the point. The grading is strict: it also turns away about a third of what would have fitted ("open my mail" for a Gmail tab), and the rows come about a second later than before, each as it is graded. The searches it suggests where the engine's aren't asked for are as before, three at most.
- The iPhone's bar fades to the page's own colour: white over a white page, dark over a dark one, navy over navy, and what the bar draws turns dark or light to read on it. It used to fade to the system's ground, so in Dark Mode a light page had a dark bar with dark controls on it. The page is looked at where the bar is, not at its top, and again as it scrolls: a quick look at the strip that meets the bar while it moves, and an exact one under the bar once it is still (WebKit's own snapshot leaves out what the bar covers). A shade's difference draws nothing again (`SiteColor.isNear`), and a change of colour eases in. Over the start page, which is the app's own, the system's appearance stands (`BarBackdrop.tint`, `Page.sampleFoot`).
- Use This Site's Own Menus and Show Link Menu on This Site take hold on the page that is up. The rule that keeps WebKit's callouts away went in only as a page loaded, so a page loaded with it showed no link menu after the switch until it was loaded again, and one loaded without it kept WebKit's picture menu. The rule is a style of its own now, which the app gives the page or takes away as the setting changes (`bridge.js` `callouts()`), so a web app keeps its state. Found by the review of the pull request.
- Other apps open from a site's own button. A page that went to another app's link by itself (an "Open in app" button whose script sets the address, `window.open`, a timer) was dropped without a word, and one asked for in a new window left an empty tab behind and opened nothing; only a link tapped directly opened its app. The page now asks first, as Safari does: "Open in Another App?", naming the site and the link's scheme, and Open hands the link over. A tapped link still opens at once, and a universal link tapped on another site is WebKit's to open, as before. A frame inside the page and a tab that isn't on screen are never let ask, one question shows at a time, and a second Cancel stands until the page changes, so a page that keeps trying asks twice at most (`AppLink.swift`). A window opened only to go to another app closes again, and when nothing on the device opens the link the app says so.
- Importing a bookmarks file that ends inside a tag (a download cut short) no longer stops the app: the reader ran past the end of the file (`BookmarkFile`).
- A window restored with a tab that has been closed since shows the tab its space is on, rather than "Nothing open" over a space that has tabs.
- The command bar's rows for another device's tabs are told apart by their place, so two tabs on one address are two rows rather than one identity SwiftUI draws twice.
- Settings › App icon's pictures show on iPad: they were kept at 3x only, which an iPad, at 2x, does not load, so each is kept once for every scale.
- The tabs from other devices in the tab overview show an iPad's tabs under an iPad, not an iPhone.
- Settings on iPhone: the address bar's two choices are rows with a checkmark; the menu beside the long label wrapped onto a line of its own.
- iCloud sync: a change made on this device and not yet sent survives a fetch of another device's version of the same record; it goes out on top of that version instead of being put back.
- iCloud sync: a device offline when sync first started joins when it next comes to the front, rather than at the next launch.
- `project.yml`, which makes the Xcode project, carries the build number and the alternate icons, so regenerating it no longer takes the build number back to 7.
- Tab in a page's field moves to the next field again. iPadOS 26's focus system keeps Tab from the page, so nothing moved; Tab now reaches the page first on every site, as it did only where an adapter or Settings asked, and the page script moves the focus when the page doesn't: through tab order, and from a field inside a shadow root to the next field after it rather than the page's first.
- The row of tabs lays itself out again when the first ordinary tab is pinned or the last pinned tab unpinned: the order stayed the same, so the tab kept its old width until something else moved.
- A trackpad's pointer over a round or capsule button on the bars (new tab, back, forward, the space, close, the star, reload) lit up a square; it now takes the shape drawn.
- A right-click (a trackpad's secondary click) on a tab opened no menu: the tab's tap answered the click first. The row of tabs is UIKit now (TabStrip.swift), where a tab's tap answers the primary button alone and the click goes on to the menu; the pointer's highlight takes the tab's shape and follows the row as it scrolls, and the close button, the star and reload are controls of their own inside the tab.
- Typing an address on an iPhone stopped at once: select all, sent to whatever had the keys, reached the page, which took them back. The field now selects its own text.
- The address field left out the last letter typed until the next key: github.com typed and sent opened github.co. The Korean keyboard keeps the last letter as text still being composed, which SwiftUI's text field doesn't pass on; the field now reads what it shows, and Return commits the composition first.

### Removed

- Open tabs over iCloud: each device's open tabs no longer go to the others, and the tab overview and the address bar no longer list the other devices' tabs. Each device deletes from iCloud the open-tabs records it knows of (`Sync.retireOpenTabs`); the `DeviceTabs` record type stays in the schema, unused. A new tab's From Other Devices lists the pages they went to instead, through History in iCloud.
- The iPad's separate layout (Settings › Window › Layout), the address bar under the row of tabs: the tabs are along the top, the tab on screen the address, or in a sidebar. A layout saved before is let go (`Preferences`).
- Safience Sync for Chrome (`Extension/`). It needed a CloudKit token only the container's owner could make, so it was for developers only, and Safience for Mac now syncs through iCloud as the iPhone and iPad do. The bookmarks and pinned tabs it synced are the space's and stay; a Chrome profile's open tabs left in iCloud stop showing 14 days after it last synced (`SyncDeviceTabs.stale`).

- The command palette as a screen of its own, with its dimmed backdrop, its looser matching (letters in order anywhere in a name, `PaletteSearch.swift`, gone with its tests) and its button at the end of the tab row: the command bar does its work. ⌃⌥K still opens it.
- Typing the address in the tab row itself on iPad (the tab on screen widening into a field) and in the separate layout's address bar: an address is typed in the command bar.

- The Mac app this repository began as, Search by Office Commun: its sources, build and release scripts, tests, AI engine, installer, roadmap and release notes. It stays in the git history.
