# Privacy Policy

**Safience** for iPhone, iPad and Mac · Effective October 23, 2026

Safience is a web browser made by Taehee Jung. This policy says what the app does with your information. In short: **Safience collects nothing.** It has no account, no server, no analytics, no advertising and no tracking. What it keeps stays on your device, or in your own iCloud account if you turn on iCloud sync.

## What Safience collects

Nothing. The developer receives no data from the app: not what you browse, not what you search, not how you use the app. There is no sign-up, and the app contains no analytics, advertising or tracking code from anyone.

## What stays on your device

To work as a browser, the app keeps some things on your iPhone, iPad or Mac. They are not sent to the developer or anyone else.

| What | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Your spaces, their tabs and their bookmarks | So your tabs and bookmarks are there the next time you open the app |
| Your settings | So the app works the way you set it |
| Your browsing history: the pages you go to in each space, when, their titles and what they say they are about (their description), and the searches you typed in the address bar | So you can find a page again in History and the address bar. Each space keeps its own. Kept for a month unless you change it in Settings › History, and cleared there or from History's Clear. It leaves the device only if you turn on Sync history in Settings › iCloud (below). Never kept: sign-in pages, a page that asks for a password, an address that carries a sign-in, and a page opened in a pop-up |
| Each space's website data: cookies, sign-ins and what sites store | So you stay signed in to the sites you use. Each space keeps its own, apart from the others. WebKit, Apple's web engine, keeps this data; the app itself never reads your cookies |
| Pictures of tabs that are put to sleep to save memory | So a sleeping tab looks the way you left it while it loads again. A sign-in page is never saved as a picture |
| Sites' icons | To show next to tabs and bookmarks |
| Ad-blocking lists, and the sites you allowed ads on | To block ads and trackers as pages load |
| A page you hand off, when you use Send to App (Settings › Send to App, off unless you turn it on) | Written as a file in a temporary folder so it can go to the app you pick; removed at your next handoff, or at once if no app took it. Where Apple Intelligence is on (iOS and iPadOS 26 and later), Apple's model on your device reads the page's title, description, headings and the start of what is under each to tell what kind of page it is and say what it is about; that is not kept, and not sent anywhere. For a video on YouTube the app also reads the video's transcript from YouTube's own page, to put it in the file |
| Download activity and files | File names, source addresses, the originating space, progress and resume information stay on this device so interrupted downloads can be retried. On iPhone and iPad, files remain in the app until you save them to Files. On Mac, files go to Downloads. Clearing activity does not delete downloaded files |
| Bookmarks you import | Read from the file you pick, on your device |
| What you type in the address bar, with the names and sites of your tabs and bookmarks and the titles, sites and descriptions of pages in your history, when Apple Intelligence suggests from them; and what you type in History's search when you press Return | Read by Apple's model on your device (Settings › Search › Suggest with Apple Intelligence, on iOS and iPadOS 26 and later where Apple Intelligence is on), to find what you mean; not kept, and not sent anywhere |

iOS, iPadOS and macOS may include the app's data in your device's backups (iCloud Backup, Time Machine), as they do for any app, under your backup settings.

## What leaves your device

Only what using a browser needs:

- **The sites you visit.** Pages load from the sites you open, as in any browser. Those sites see your visits and may collect information under their own privacy policies.
- **Your searches.** Words you type that aren't an address go to the search engine you choose in Settings (Google unless you change it), under that engine's privacy policy.
- **Search suggestions.** After you explicitly enable Settings › Search › Search engine suggestions for the selected provider (off by default), what you type in the address bar goes to that search engine as you type, so it can suggest searches, if the engine offers them (Google, DuckDuckGo and Bing do). Complete addresses are excluded once recognized, but a partial address can be sent before it is recognized. The requests carry no cookies or sign-ins. Changing the search provider turns suggestions off until you enable them for that provider. Turn search engine suggestions off to keep address-bar typing on your device. The suggestions from your tabs, bookmarks and the app's commands are made on your device and go nowhere.
- **Sites' icons.** The app downloads the icon a page you visited names, without sending your cookies or sign-ins.
- **Ad-blocking lists.** With ad blocking on (Settings › Ads and trackers, on unless you turn it off), the app downloads the filter lists that are turned on in Settings › Ads and trackers › Filter lists, each from its authors' server (easylist.to, easylist-downloads.adblockplus.org, secure.fanboy.co.nz or raw.githubusercontent.com), at most once every four days, and only if it changed. A request carries no cookies and nothing about you or what you browse; like any server, these see your device's IP address. The blocking itself happens on your device.
- **A page you hand off**, if you turn on Send to App (Settings › Send to App, off unless you turn it on). When you choose Send Page to App for a page and then pick an app in the share sheet, that app gets a file with the page's text (first, anything you had selected on the page), the page's address and the time the page was read, and a line for your question. The text is the page as it showed to you: if you are signed in to a site, what it shows you alone is part of it. The address goes without the tags that say how you came to the page (`utm_source`, `fbclid` and the like), and the time is by Greenwich's clock, so it does not say which time zone you are in. It goes to the app you pick and nowhere else, under that app's privacy policy, and nothing goes until you pick one. For a video on YouTube the file also has the video's transcript, which the app reads from YouTube's own page. On an iPhone, where YouTube's page shows no transcript, the app loads the video's page at youtube.com a second time, out of sight, as you are signed in or not on that site, reads the transcript there and closes it: YouTube sees that as another visit to the video's page. Nothing is played, and the app asks YouTube for nothing else.
- **Your spaces, bookmarks and pinned tabs, if you turn on iCloud sync** (Settings › iCloud, off unless you turn it on). Each space's name, colour, icon, bookmarks and pinned tabs are kept in your own iCloud account by Apple (CloudKit's private database), under Apple's privacy policy, so they show on your other iPhones, iPads and Macs. The developer can't read them. If the Apple Account changes, workspace uploads pause until you choose whether to sync retained local data with the current account. Your open tabs stay on each device and are not synced, and neither are your sign-ins, cookies and what sites store.
- **Your history, if you turn on Sync history in Settings › iCloud** (off unless you turn it on, and only with iCloud sync on). The pages you went to in each space, when, their titles and descriptions, and the searches you typed in the address bar, are kept in your own iCloud account by Apple in CloudKit's encrypted fields: encrypted in transit and on Apple's servers, and end to end with Advanced Data Protection on. Eligible visits show in History and the address bar on your other iPhones, iPads and Macs. Each new local visit captures its approved account scope when recorded; that scope does not change when a failed write is retried. Visits saved before approval, while account verification is unavailable or while main iCloud sync is off stay local. Older untagged history remains on the device and is not newly uploaded after upgrading. Previously synced records are retained until clearing or normal retention. Titles and descriptions shared with a visit are captured within the same approved account scope. The developer can't read them. Clearing history on one device clears it on all of them: the clearing itself is kept in iCloud for at most 400 days, so a device that was offline clears it too, and a page cleared is named in it only by a one-way digest, never by its address. Turning Sync history off deletes that device's history from iCloud; what your other devices already have stays on them. iCloud keeps no history older than 400 days.

## On a Mac: Chrome extensions

Safience for Mac runs Chrome extensions you add. An extension is someone else's software: what it does with what it can read is under its developer's privacy policy, not this one. On the sites you allow it, an extension can read and change the page as the page's own scripts can, and send what it reads elsewhere. Safience limits what any extension may reach: none gets Chrome's cookies API, none runs on Google's sign-in (accounts.google.com), and each reads only the sites you allowed it when you added it, or only the page you click it on.

What the app keeps on your Mac for them, sent to no one:

| What | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Each extension's files, as they came and as prepared to run | To run it |
| What each extension stores, kept apart for each space it is on in | Its settings and sign-ins, as it keeps them. Removing it from a space deletes them there |
| What an extension ran into: which of its features failed, where in it, how often, when it first and last happened, and the versions of the extension, Safience and macOS. Never an error's words, a page or an address | To say when something doesn't work, and for a report if you choose to send one. At most 200 an extension, for 30 days, deleted with the extension |
| The results of trying an extension's pages when it was added, in a temporary space that keeps no data | So they aren't tried again until something changes |
| Messages between an extension and a desktop app on your Mac that goes with it (Bitwarden's, 1Password's), through that app's helper | So the extension can work with the app. They stay between the two on your Mac; what the app does next is under its own policy. If the extension didn't ask for this when you added it, Safience asks you first |

What leaves your Mac for them:

- **The extensions you add, and their updates**, downloaded from Google's Chrome Web Store, which sees each download as any store does.
- **A check for updates about once a day** (Settings › Extensions, on unless you turn it off; again within the hour if it fails; Check Now checks at once): the ids and versions of your Chrome Web Store extensions go to Google's update server, as Chrome's check does, so it can say which have a newer version.
- **What an extension sends itself.** Its pages and worker reach the servers it uses, under its developer's policy. Its worker's WebSocket connections go through Safience, to the server the extension names, with no cookies but with whatever the extension sends. When an extension is added, its pages are tried once in a temporary space, and can reach those servers then too.
- **The list of your Chrome Web Store extensions**, if iCloud sync is on: each one's store id, name and site access, and the spaces it is on in, kept in your own iCloud account in encrypted fields (end to end if you turn on Advanced Data Protection), so your other Macs can offer them. The developer can't read it. Removing an extension on one Mac removes it, with what it kept, on your other Macs. Extensions added from a file or a folder are left out.
- **A report, only if you send it.** Report a Problem… shows everything in it; you copy or save it and send it where you like. Safience sends nothing.

## Permissions

The app asks the system for these only when they are needed, and you can turn each off in the Settings app at any time:

- **Camera and microphone**, when a site you use asks for them, for a video call for example. They go to that site, never to the developer.
- **Files**, only for a file you choose: a bookmarks file to import, where to save a download, or on a Mac an extension to add. On a Mac, Safience runs outside the App Sandbox, so that extensions can reach the desktop apps that go with them.

Passwords, passkeys and verification codes are handled by Apple's Passwords app and AutoFill. Safience does not store them.

## Crash reports

If you choose to share analytics with app developers (Settings › Privacy & Security › Analytics & Improvements), Apple may share crash reports with the developer to help fix problems. You can turn this off at any time.

## Deleting your data

- Remove a space to delete its tabs, bookmarks and website data, its sign-ins included.
- Close a tab to delete its page and its picture. The addresses of the last 25 closed tabs are kept, so you can reopen them.
- Clear History (in History, or Settings › History) to delete your history from the last hour, today, today and yesterday, or all of it. With Sync history on in Settings › iCloud, it is cleared on your other devices and in iCloud too.
- Delete the app to delete everything it kept on an iPhone or iPad. On a Mac, deleting the app leaves its folders: delete Safience's folders in your Library's Application Support, Caches and WebKit folders, and its preferences, too. Spaces, bookmarks and pinned tabs you remove while iCloud sync is on are removed from iCloud too: each is kept there, marked removed, for 30 days, so your other devices remove it as well, and then deleted.
- On a Mac, remove an extension from Safience to delete its files, what it kept in every space, and its records.
